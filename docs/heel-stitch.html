Heel Stitch

Slipped stitches make a thick, ribbed fabric that wears well. Traditionally used for sock heels, but just as good for a hard-wearing bag or a cosy mug cosy.

Cast on a multiple of 2 stitches. The pattern repeats every 2 rows. Read the chart from the bottom up. Right-side (odd) rows are read from right to left, wrong-side (even) rows from left to right.

Key

Knit on right-side rows; purl on wrong-side rows.
Slip 1: slip the next stitch purlwise to the right needle without working it, with the yarn at the back (on a wrong-side row, with the yarn at the front, so the strand stays on the wrong side).

Instructions

Row 1 (RS)*sl1 wyib, k1; repeat from * to end.
Row 2 (WS)Purl.

Repeat rows 1–2 for the length you need.

Notes

Slip the stitches purlwise with the yarn at the back, so the strand lies on the wrong side.

Slipped stitches pull the fabric up, so heel stitch is shorter and denser than stocking stitch.

For a sock heel, work it back and forth over half the stitches.
