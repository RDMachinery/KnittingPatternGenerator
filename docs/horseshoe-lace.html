Horseshoe Lace

A traditional Shetland lace of rounded arches, like rows of horseshoes. Each arch narrows to a central double decrease. A favourite for shawls and stoles.

Cast on a multiple of 10 stitches. The pattern repeats every 8 rows. Read the chart from the bottom up. Right-side (odd) rows are read from right to left, wrong-side (even) rows from left to right.

Key

Knit on right-side rows; purl on wrong-side rows.
Purl on right-side rows; knit on wrong-side rows.
Yarn over: take the yarn over the needle to make a new stitch and a small decorative hole.
SK2P: slip 1 knitwise, k2tog, then pass the slipped stitch over. A double decrease: 3 stitches become 1.

Instructions

Row 1 (RS)*yo, k3, sk2p, k3, yo, k1; repeat from * to end.
Row 2 (WS)Purl.
Row 3 (RS)*k1, yo, k2, sk2p, k2, yo, k1, p1; repeat from * to end.
Row 4 (WS)*k1, p9; repeat from * to end.
Row 5 (RS)*k2, yo, k1, sk2p, k1, yo, k2, p1; repeat from * to end.
Row 6 (WS)As row 4.
Row 7 (RS)*k3, yo, sk2p, yo, k4; repeat from * to end.
Row 8 (WS)As row 2.

Repeat rows 1–8 for the length you need.

Notes

Every right-side row is balanced: two yarn overs replace the two stitches lost to the SK2P, so the stitch count never changes.

For a symmetrical edge, cast on a multiple of 10 plus 1, and work the extra stitch at the end of each row like the left-hand square of the chart (the column with the purl bumps).

Use a smooth yarn and block firmly to open the arches.
